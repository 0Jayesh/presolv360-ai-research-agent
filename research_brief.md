# Research Brief: The impact of AI on jobs and employment

## Executive Summary

- **Dimensions analyzed:** 10
- **Consensus findings:** 2
- **Contradictory findings:** 0
- **Outlier findings:** 3
- **Research gaps:** 9

## Findings by Dimension

### Net job displacement versus creation estimates

#### Gap

Lack of standardized long-term empirical data tracking global or national net employment gains versus displacement over identical timeframes.

### Sectors and occupations most exposed to AI automation

#### Gap

Insufficient cross-country comparative sector data examining automation exposure outside North American and European markets.

### Skill shifts and reskilling requirements

#### Gap

Lack of detailed metrics regarding the direct costs, average training duration, and institutional efficacy of enterprise reskilling programs.

### Impact on wages and income inequality

#### Gap

Absence of longitudinal data tracking how AI implementation impacts compensation structures across middle- and low-wage tiers over time.

### Timeline and rate of AI adoption in the workforce

#### Gap

Lack of unified metrics for measuring active AI integration versus trial or passive software adoption across industries.

### Geographic and regional labor market variations

#### Outliers

- **Workers facing both high AI exposure and low adaptive capacity are concentrated in smaller metropolitan areas, university towns, and state capitals, particularly in the Mountain West and Midwest.**
  - Evidence: The concentration of exposed and vulnerable workers is greatest in smaller metro areas and college towns, particularly in the Mountain West and Midwest—reflecting such areas’ elevated presence of administrative and clerical workers.
  - Source: https://www.brookings.edu/articles/measuring-us-workers-capacity-to-adapt-to-ai-driven-job-displacement/
  - Citation: Findings: On average, highly AI-exposed workers appear well-equipped to handle job transitions relative to the rest of the workforce, yet 6.1 million workers still face both high exposure and low adaptive capacity, paragraph 6

#### Gap

Lack of empirical data on geographic and regional labor market variations outside the United States.

### Effects on specific demographic groups

#### Consensus

- **Unemployment among recent college graduates has risen to nearly 6%, growing twice as fast as the rest of the workforce since 2022.**
  - Evidence: unemployment among recent graduates has climbed to nearly 6%, rising twice as fast as the rest of the workforce since 2022.
  - Source: https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/the-real-job-destruction-from-ai-is-hitting-before-careers-can-start
  - Citation: Section: The Real Job Destruction from AI Is Hitting Before Careers Can Start

- **College graduates have seen rising unemployment rates, with those holding AI-exposed majors like computer engineering, graphic design, industrial engineering, and architecture experiencing significant increases.**
  - Evidence: For starters, the unemployment rate among college graduates has increased, reaching 5.8% in March — the highest in more than four years. ... However, other majors exposed to AI, including computer engineering, graphic design, industrial engineering and architecture, have also experienced significant increases in graduate unemployment
  - Source: https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/artificial-intelligence/ai-impact-job-growth
  - Citation: Section: Unemployment among college graduates is increasing, paragraphs 2 and 3

#### Outliers

- **Women comprise 86% of the 6.1 million workers in occupations characterized by high AI exposure and low adaptive capacity.**
  - Evidence: At the same time, 6.1 million workers, primarily in clerical and administrative roles, lack adaptive capacity due to limited savings, advanced age, scarce local opportunities, and/or narrow skill sets. Of these workers, 86% are women.
  - Source: https://www.brookings.edu/articles/measuring-us-workers-capacity-to-adapt-to-ai-driven-job-displacement/
  - Citation: Executive Summary / Lead Paragraph, paragraph 2

#### Gap

Lack of granular demographic analysis focusing on racial, ethnic, or socioeconomic subgroups beyond gender and education level.

### Job quality, autonomy, and working conditions

#### Gap

Lack of quantitative studies assessing physical health, stress levels, workplace surveillance, and worker autonomy under AI management.

### Policy and legislative intervention recommendations

#### Outliers

- **Policymakers should use adaptive capacity measures to target public funding, workforce transition assistance, and specialized data collection toward workers with the lowest capacity to manage displacement.**
  - Evidence: Additionally, such measures could be considered to target and streamline eligibility for particular workforce transition assistance programs.
  - Source: https://www.brookings.edu/articles/measuring-us-workers-capacity-to-adapt-to-ai-driven-job-displacement/
  - Citation: Conclusion: Adaptability analysis can help reveal who may be most in need of support to weather AI-driven job transitions, paragraph 4

#### Gap

Absence of evaluations concerning specific statutory frameworks, unemployment safety-net reforms, or AI taxation policies.
